\subsection*{S10. Window-Length Robustness}
Window-length sensitivity assessed the four headline metrics using 30-, 60-, 120-, and 180-s windows from the same recordings. Paired effects were defined as $\Delta=\mathrm{Drowsy}-\mathrm{Awake}$ and summarized by the median across 20 paired recording sessions at each duration (Table~\ref{tab:supp_window}). Confidence intervals (CIs) are the stored 95\% paired-session percentile bootstrap intervals; raw signed-rank $p$ values and rank-biserial effects $r_{\mathrm{rb}}$ are reported separately. Direction counts describe sessions with a decrease for Mean CC, DET, and LLE or an increase for Mean NRMSE. This sensitivity shares the primary recordings and is not an independent replication.

Mean CC, DET, and LLE had negative median effects and Mean NRMSE had positive median effects at all four durations. Magnitudes differed across durations. The 30-s estimates generally had greater uncertainty, while longer windows generally reduced uncertainty at the cost of fewer eligible windows; the 180-s DET interval remained relatively wide. DET CIs included zero at 30 and 180 s, and the 180-s DET raw p value was 0.053169. Thus, direction preservation did not imply uniform statistical support. The 60-s configuration provided a practical balance between observation duration and uncertainty within this dataset, rather than a universal optimum. No BH-adjusted q values are reported for this duration analysis.

\subsection*{S11. Label Sensitivity}
Alternative definitions tested transition exclusion and minimum same-state episode duration. P0 used the primary recorded labels; T30 and T60 excluded samples within $\pm$30 and $\pm$60 s of transitions, respectively; S3 and S5 retained same-state episodes lasting at least 180 and 300 s. The retained windows were constructed and screened under each rule, so these cohorts need not be nested subsets of P0. Table~\ref{tab:supp_label}A reports state-specific general-QC retention. P0, T30, T60, and S3 had 20 paired sessions; S5 had 19. LLE additionally used its accepted-fit screen.

The four median-effect directions were preserved across all alternative rules, with BH correction applied across the four headline metrics separately within each alternative rule. All 16 alternative-rule tests had $q_{\mathrm{BH}}<0.05$, but magnitudes were not identical. The T60 Mean CC CI $[-0.0561,+0.0059]$ and T30 LLE CI $[-0.0535,+0.0020]$ included zero. Percentile CIs for the median paired effect and rank-based signed-rank/BH support summarize different properties and need not align exactly. P0 remains the fixed manuscript reference with its seven-metric primary family (Table~\ref{tab:supp_primary}).

\subsection*{S12. Reconstruction and Estimator Sensitivity}
Sensitivity analyses perturbed one reconstruction or estimator setting at a time while retaining the nominal configuration for other choices. They assess dependence on tested settings in the same recordings. They do not establish parameter independence or independent replication.

\subsubsection*{S12.1 Embedding Delay and Dimension}
Delay was tested at $\tau=0.12,0.16,0.20$ s with $m=8$, and dimension at $m=7,8,9$ with $\tau=0.16$ s (Table~\ref{tab:supp_reconstruction}). Mean CC, Mean NRMSE, and LLE preserved their negative, positive, and negative median-effect directions, respectively, across the tested delays. DET was delay-sensitive: its nominal decrease ($-0.0186$) became close to zero and positive at 0.12 s ($+0.0025$) and 0.20 s ($+0.0015$), with both non-nominal CIs spanning zero and no BH support. DET therefore cannot be described as uniformly stable to embedding delay.

All four median-effect directions were preserved across $m=7$--9, but magnitudes varied, including a meaningful change in the LLE decrease. DET did not meet the four-metric BH criterion at $m=7$ ($q=0.053169$) or $m=9$ ($q=0.058258$), despite negative effects and CIs excluding zero. Some Mean CC and Mean NRMSE CIs also crossed zero under reconstruction perturbations. The stored BH family comprised four headline metrics within each alternative setting. Nominal rows reproduce the fixed manuscript statistics from Table~\ref{tab:supp_primary}, with their original seven-metric q values explicitly identified; they are not replaced by sensitivity-family nominal statistics.

\subsubsection*{S12.2 RQA Estimator Settings}
RQA sensitivity tested target recurrence rates $\mathrm{RR}=0.01,0.02,0.03$ and temporal-exclusion (Theiler) multipliers 0.75, 1.00, and 1.25 relative to nominal $W=(m-1)d$, with $d$ the delay in samples. All settings retained 901 windows and 20 paired sessions. Stored sensitivity inference covers DET, LAM, and TT only (Table~\ref{tab:supp_estimator}A); raw p values are reported without BH correction. DET remained negative across these RR and Theiler perturbations. At RR=0.01 its CI included zero and raw p was 0.082550; thus its direction was stable across tested RQA settings, while statistical support varied. This contrasts with the DET sign changes under delay perturbations in S12.1.

LAM and TT had positive median effects but weaker support: their CIs included zero and their raw p values exceeded 0.05 in every tested RQA setting. They remain secondary findings. $L_{\mathrm{mean}}$ estimator-sensitivity inference was not stored. DET describes recurrence-line organization rather than physical determinism.

\subsubsection*{S12.3 LLE Estimator Settings}
LLE sensitivity tested fit intervals 0.60--1.10, 0.80--1.30 (nominal), and 1.00--1.50 s; fit-quality thresholds $R^2\geq0.90$ and $R^2\geq0.95$; and Theiler multipliers 0.75, 1.00, and 1.25 relative to its nominal spectral-mean-period exclusion (S7). All settings retained 20 paired sessions. Every median effect remained negative, each stored CI excluded zero, and all raw p values were below 0.05 (Table~\ref{tab:supp_estimator}B). No BH correction was applied across these LLE settings.

Fit intervals retained 802, 872, and 889 of 901 input windows, respectively. Raising the $R^2$ threshold from 0.90 to 0.95 retained 734 rather than 872 windows and reduced the median decrease. The 0.75 and 1.00 Theiler multipliers yielded the same stored effect and retention; the 1.25 multiplier retained 874 windows and changed the effect magnitude. Direction consistency therefore coexists with changes in magnitude and retained-window count; the estimator is not parameter-independent. These finite-window divergence estimates do not establish deterministic chaos.

\subsection*{S13. Unknown Repeated-Session Dependence}
The actual participant--session mapping was unavailable. A hypothetical dependence sensitivity analysis grouped the 20 recording sessions into 10 two-session clusters using random perfect matchings. Sampling with replacement drew random permutations of the 20 sessions and paired adjacent entries, producing 100,000 sampled matchings and 99,990 unique matchings out of 654,729,075 theoretically possible perfect matchings. These hypothetical clusters do not recover participant identity or establish actual session independence.

For each matching, the cluster difference was the arithmetic mean of its two session differences. The metric effect was the median of the 10 cluster differences. A two-sided conditional signed-rank test discarded differences within $10^{-12}$ of zero and used average ranks for absolute-value ties; rank-biserial effects used the positive and negative rank sums. BH correction was applied across all seven metrics within each matching. No bootstrap was performed inside matchings. Table~\ref{tab:supp_repeated}A reports the resulting hypothetical matching distribution; its 2.5th--97.5th percentile ranges describe variation across matchings and are not bootstrap CIs.

All four headline median-effect directions were preserved in every sampled matching, while the fractions with $q_{\mathrm{BH}}<0.05$ were 19.36\% for Mean CC, 27.82\% for Mean NRMSE, 22.59\% for DET, and 79.69\% for LLE. Direction was more stable than inferential support when the number of effective units was reduced to 10 hypothetical clusters. LLE retained the strongest inferential support within this analysis. These frequencies describe sampled hypothetical matchings under the specified design; they are not probabilities that the true result is significant and do not support participant-level inference. The saved 50,000- and 100,000-draw summaries were similar (Table~\ref{tab:supp_repeated}B), indicating Monte Carlo stability for the sampled design without resolving the unknown linkage.

\subsection*{S14. Symbolic Dynamics}
An exploratory supporting analysis encoded PPG-derived peak-to-peak intervals (PPI) at 60, 120, and 180 s. Peaks were detected in each processed waveform without extra smoothing, using minimum separation $\lceil0.30 f_s\rceil$ samples and prominence 0.20 times its 95th--5th percentile amplitude range. PPI equaled successive peak-index differences divided by $f_s$. Accepted windows required finite PPI, no intervals outside 0.30--2.00 s, peak-span coverage of at least 90\% of the window, and at least $\max\{3,\lceil T/2\rceil-2\}$ intervals for duration $T$ in seconds. Relative changes above 0.20 were diagnostic and did not cause rejection.

Within each accepted window, PPI was quantized into six equal-width levels between its minimum and maximum; internal-edge values entered the upper bin, and a constant sequence used level zero. Overlapping three-symbol words were assigned to 0V when both adjacent differences were zero, 1V when exactly one was zero, and 2V when both were nonzero. The 2V category combined same-sign (2LV) and opposite-sign (2UV) nonzero changes. Percentages were occurrence proportions among all overlapping words. The three category proportions sum to 100\% within a window, although separate session-state medians need not. Session-state summaries were medians across accepted windows, followed by paired session comparisons.

Table~\ref{tab:supp_symbolic} retains all nine category-by-duration results and the stored BH correction across three categories within each duration. Median 0V effects were positive and 2V effects negative at every duration, while 1V was close to zero and changed aggregate sign at 180 s. None of the nine tests met its within-duration BH criterion. The 60-s 0V CI and 180-s 2V CI excluded zero; the latter raw p was 0.048441, but its q was 0.145323. These uncertainty and raw-test features do not override the exploratory multiplicity result.

The within-session paired effect had the same sign across all three durations in 17/20 sessions for 0V, 11/20 for 1V, and 14/20 for 2V. These counts include consistently positive or consistently negative sessions and describe sign consistency only; they neither require the aggregate expected direction nor establish equal magnitude across durations. PPG-derived symbolic occurrence proportions provide directional context and are not interpreted as direct sympathetic/vagal balance or as validated ECG-derived autonomic measures. They are outside the primary four-metric claim.

\subsection*{S15. Overall Robustness Summary}
Table~\ref{tab:supp_summary} separates the fixed nominal results, preservation of effect directions, uncertainty, and variation in statistical support. Mean CC decreased and Mean NRMSE increased under the nominal configuration, with those directions maintained across tested window lengths, label rules, delays, and dimensions. Magnitudes and CIs varied across settings, and hypothetical grouping attenuated BH support. These patterns concern finite-horizon forecastability within the tested configurations.

DET decreased nominally and retained its negative direction across window lengths, label rules, and dimensions, as well as the tested RR/Theiler RQA settings. Its near-zero positive effects at non-nominal delays make embedding-delay dependence an explicit qualification. Neither this nominal decrease nor direction stability under other settings establishes physical determinism. Statistical support varied with settings and was attenuated under hypothetical grouping.

LLE decreased across the tested window, label, reconstruction, and estimator settings. It retained the strongest inferential support in the hypothetical-grouping analysis, while its effect magnitude and number of accepted windows varied with fit and quality settings. This describes a finite-window local-divergence estimate, not an estimator independent of parameter choices. Symbolic dynamics remained exploratory and offered directional context outside the primary four-metric claim.

The sensitivity branches reuse the same recordings and are supportive analyses rather than independent replications. Unknown participant--session linkage remains a limitation: hypothetical repeated-session grouping does not restore that mapping or yield participant-level inference. No pooled significance, robustness score, or ranking of metrics is inferred from the evidence matrix.
