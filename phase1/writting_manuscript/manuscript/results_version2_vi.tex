\section{Results}

The primary analysis was performed on valid $60$-s processed-PPG windows that satisfied the predefined quality-control criteria. The following results first examine the dynamical organization of the signal relative to the noisy pseudoperiodic null and then compare the dynamical properties of the Awake and Drowsy states.


\subsection{Dynamical Organization Beyond a Noisy Pseudoperiodic Null}

In both Awake and Drowsy states, the original PPG differed from PPS in a generally consistent pattern across the dynamical measures. For finite-horizon forecastability, the original PPG showed higher Mean CC and lower Mean NRMSE than the surrogate ensemble. DET and $L_{\mathrm{mean}}$ were also higher, indicating stronger and more persistent diagonal recurrence structures relative to PPS; LLE was higher as well, whereas LAM and TT were generally lower. Overall, these differences indicate that the original signal and the noisy pseudoperiodic ensemble differed across prediction, recurrence structure, and local trajectory divergence.

Fig.~\ref{fig:rq1_evidence} summarizes the session-level original-minus-PPS deviations together with the fraction of windows rejecting the PPS null. The median session-level rejection fraction for Mean CC was $60.7\%$ in Awake and $71.0\%$ in Drowsy. For DET, the median rejection fraction reached $100\%$ in both states.

Rejection of the PPS null indicates that the observed PPG is inconsistent with the null hypothesis of a periodic orbit with uncorrelated noise~\cite{small2001}. This suggests that the signal contains dynamical organization beyond simple noisy pseudoperiodicity, but it does not constitute evidence of deterministic chaos and does not exclude more complex pseudoperiodic dynamics with correlated structure. The rejection fractions describe evidence against the null within each state and were not used as a paired test between Awake and Drowsy.

\subsection{Awake--Drowsy Differences in PPG Dynamics}

Table~\ref{tab:awake_drowsy_statistics} summarizes the paired effects of the seven metrics in the primary $60$-s analysis, while Fig.~\ref{fig:awake_drowsy_paired} illustrates the session-level changes from Awake to Drowsy. The headline metrics showed a coordinated pattern in the Drowsy state: Mean CC decreased, Mean NRMSE increased, DET decreased, and LLE decreased. For finite-horizon forecastability, Mean CC decreased with a median paired difference of $\Delta=-0.0339$ and a $95\%$ CI of $[-0.0595,-0.0043]$, whereas Mean NRMSE increased with $\Delta=+0.0294$ and a $95\%$ CI of $[0.0047,0.0530]$. Both remained statistically supported after BH-FDR correction, with $q_{\mathrm{BH}}=0.0376$ for Mean CC and $q_{\mathrm{BH}}=0.0255$ for Mean NRMSE, indicating lower finite-horizon forecastability in the Drowsy state.

For recurrence organization, DET decreased with $\Delta=-0.0186$, a $95\%$ CI of $[-0.0364,-0.0069]$, and $q_{\mathrm{BH}}=0.0376$, indicating lower diagonal recurrence organization in the Drowsy state under the nominal reconstruction configuration. $L_{\mathrm{mean}}$ also shifted in the same direction, with $\Delta=-0.0630$ and a CI that did not include $0$, but it was no longer supported after BH-FDR correction ($q_{\mathrm{BH}}=0.0816$). In contrast, LAM and TT tended to increase, but their confidence intervals included $0$, and the corresponding effects were not supported after correction for multiple comparisons. These vertical or laminar recurrence changes were therefore treated as secondary and were less consistent than the change in DET.

For local trajectory divergence, LLE decreased with $\Delta=-0.0376~\mathrm{s}^{-1}$, a $95\%$ CI of $[-0.0537,-0.0230]~\mathrm{s}^{-1}$, $r_{\mathrm{rb}}=-0.810$, and $q_{\mathrm{BH}}=0.0050$, indicating lower estimated local trajectory divergence in the Drowsy state. Overall, RQ2 indicated that drowsiness was associated with coordinated changes across three complementary properties of PPG dynamics: lower finite-horizon forecastability, lower diagonal recurrence organization, and lower estimated local trajectory divergence. The remaining metrics provided additional context but did not show statistical support as consistently as the four headline findings.

\subsection{Robustness of the Primary Findings}

Mean CC, DET, and LLE remained lower in the Drowsy state, whereas Mean NRMSE remained higher, across all four window lengths of $30$, $60$, $120$, and $180$~s (Fig.~\ref{fig:window_robustness}). The effect directions were more consistent than their magnitudes. Estimates from $30$-s windows showed greater uncertainty, whereas uncertainty was generally lower from $60$ to $180$~s, although DET at $180$~s still showed a relatively wide interval. Within this dataset, $60$~s provided a practical balance between short observation duration and estimation uncertainty rather than a universal optimum. The same four directions were also preserved under the P0, T30, T60, S3, and S5 label rules (Table~\ref{tab:label_sensitivity}), suggesting that the observed pattern was not driven primarily by windows near state transitions or by short state episodes within the tested definitions. Across the four alternative label rules, all $16/16$ headline effects retained the expected direction and had $q_{\mathrm{BH}}<0.05$, although the bootstrap CI included $0$ for T60--Mean CC and T30--LLE; S5 retained $19$ paired sessions compared with $20$ for the other rules. The bootstrap CI summarizes uncertainty in the median paired effect, whereas the signed-rank test is rank based, so the two forms of statistical support need not coincide.

Robustness to parameter choices differed across metrics (Table~\ref{tab:parameter_sensitivity}). Mean CC, Mean NRMSE, and LLE preserved their directions across the tested delay and dimension grid. DET remained negative at $m=7$, $8$, and $9$, but was clearly more sensitive to embedding delay: the negative effect at $\tau=0.16$~s approached $0$ and changed sign at $\tau=0.12$ and $0.20$~s. The DET finding should therefore be interpreted as reconstruction-setting dependent, particularly with respect to embedding delay. To assess sensitivity to unknown repeated-session dependence using the same canonical primary pipeline, $100{,}000$ hypothetical pairings were sampled by grouping the $20$ sessions into $10$ two-session clusters because the actual participant--session linkage was unavailable. The median effects of all four headline metrics preserved their directions across all sampled hypothetical pairings, but their magnitudes and inferential support varied. The proportions with $q_{\mathrm{BH}}<0.05$ were $19.36\%$, $27.82\%$, $22.59\%$, and $79.69\%$ for Mean CC, Mean NRMSE, DET, and LLE, respectively. This analysis assesses sensitivity to unknown participant--session clustering and does not substitute for the primary session-level inference. The hypothetical pairings do not recover participant identity, establish independence between sessions, or support participant-level inference.

As an exploratory supporting analysis, symbolic dynamics showed $0V\uparrow$ and $2V\downarrow$ at $60$, $120$, and $180$~s, whereas 1V remained close to $0$ and was less consistent (Table~\ref{tab:symbolic_results}; differences are reported in percentage points). This pattern provides additional context for the state-related dynamical changes, but it did not show consistent statistical support across configurations and was not interpreted as a direct measure of sympathetic or parasympathetic activity. Overall, across the robustness analyses performed, the directions of the primary effects were more stable than their exact magnitudes and levels of statistical support. Robustness was metric specific: Mean CC, Mean NRMSE, and LLE showed better directional stability across the tested parameter grid, whereas DET was more sensitive to embedding delay. The repeated-session sensitivity analysis further supported directional stability but did not remove uncertainty related to participant--session dependence.
