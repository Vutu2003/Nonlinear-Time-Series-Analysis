\section{Introduction}

Drowsiness is a gradual decline in vigilance, accompanied by reduced ability to sustain attention and respond to external stimuli~\cite{peker2026,el2026}. Photoplethysmography (PPG) is well suited for monitoring this process because it is non-invasive, low-cost, and easy to integrate into wearable devices~\cite{charlton2023}. Most PPG studies on drowsiness focus on physiological, morphological, or time--frequency features derived from the signal, including heart-rate variability (HRV), mainly for detection or classification~\cite{amidei2021,cao2023,alarnaout2025,yu2024}. However, the PPG waveform itself also contains temporal organization and nonlinear dynamical structure~\cite{de2020,sviridova2015}. This motivates a direct analysis of PPG dynamics to capture aspects of the signal that may not be fully represented by features designed for classification.

Previous nonlinear PPG studies, particularly those by Sviridova and colleagues, provide a basis for studying the signal as a nonlinear dynamical process~\cite{sviridova2015}. The near-periodic oscillatory nature of PPG also motivates pseudoperiodic surrogate (PPS) testing, in which the observed organization is compared with a specific noisy pseudoperiodic null model~\cite{sviridova2018}. Useful dynamical information can also be extracted from relatively short recordings, although the accuracy of the estimates depends on the property being measured and the data length~\cite{sviridova2022}. Building on this work, an important open question is how complementary dynamical properties of PPG reorganize across the transition from Awake to Drowsy. Addressing this question requires both testing whether the observed organization exceeds the selected null model and comparing dynamical properties between the two states.

A single measure is unlikely to fully describe this reorganization. We therefore consider three complementary views: finite-horizon forecastability, assessed using Simplex Projection; recurrence organization, characterized using Recurrence Quantification Analysis (RQA); and local trajectory divergence, estimated using the Rosenstein Largest Lyapunov Exponent (LLE). These measures describe different aspects of the dynamics, and their changes should not be reduced to a single axis of increasing or decreasing chaos or complexity. Ultra-short windows provide better temporal resolution for tracking the gradual decline in vigilance, but they also contain fewer state-space points, recurrence structures, and trajectory pairs for estimation. Because finite data increase the uncertainty of nonlinear estimates, reliability must be considered for each property and data length~\cite{shaffer2020,sviridova2022}. State comparisons should therefore be accompanied by robustness analyses across window lengths and sensitivity analyses of the main analysis settings.

This study addresses two research questions. \textbf{RQ1:} Does ultra-short PPG in the Awake and Drowsy states contain temporal or dynamical organization beyond the tested noisy pseudoperiodic null? \textbf{RQ2:} How is drowsiness associated with changes in finite-horizon forecastability, recurrence organization, and local trajectory divergence? PPS testing addresses the first question, while Awake--Drowsy comparisons based on nonlinear measures address the second. Analyses across window lengths and sensitivity tests for label definitions, analysis settings, and unknown repeated-session dependence are used to evaluate the robustness and inferential scope of the findings. The main contribution is a coordinated characterization of ultra-short PPG dynamical reorganization across the Awake--Drowsy transition using complementary nonlinear properties, together with an assessment of how robust these changes are across data lengths and the selected analysis settings.
